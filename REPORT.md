# Gait signal core — findings

Scope: **48 jog trials** (24 subjects × trials 9 and 16), MotionSense `A_DeviceMotion_data`,
50 Hz nominal, iPhone 6s in the front trouser pocket.

**Provenance — what is traceable and what is not.** Most numbers below are per-trial columns
in `reports/pipeline_results.csv` and `reports/invariance.csv`, regenerated by
`scripts/run_all.py` and `scripts/run_invariance_checks.py`: cadence and its diagnosis, the
stage-2 verdicts, tilt, step and stride regularity, the symmetry index, alternation and its
null models, and both invariance sweeps. Re-running those two scripts reproduces every one
of them.

Four groups of numbers are **not** in either CSV and are not regenerated by either script,
because the underlying quantities are not among the columns `pipeline.flatten` emits:

- the broadband-PCA vs step-band conditioning comparison (§3),
- the second-harmonic power ratio (§4),
- the static-vs-tracking vertical correlation (§3),
- the low-pass cutoff sweep and the ensemble peaks-per-cycle figure (§4).

These come from one-off analyses over the same 48 trials. The harmonic-rejection claim they
support is pinned by `tests/test_failure_modes.py::test_lowpass_rejects_the_second_harmonic_on_filter_shape_alone`;
the rest are reproducible only by re-running the analysis by hand. Treat them as measured
but not continuously verified.

## 2. Stage 1 — loader and explorer

Layout was read from the dataset's own README and then verified on disk before any loader
code was written: `A_DeviceMotion_data/<activity>_<trial>/sub_<subject>.csv`, jog = trials
9 and 16, 24 subjects each, 12 signal columns plus an unnamed integer row counter.

**The one irregularity that matters is the one that cannot be measured.** There is no
timestamp column anywhere in this dataset. The time index is `arange(n) / fs_hz` — a grid
we impose. Dropped samples, clock jitter and rate drift are all undetectable. A 97.2 s
trial and a 100 s trial that lost 140 samples are byte-identical to this loader. Every
integrity record carries `has_timestamp_column: False` and
`timestamp_irregularities_detectable: False` so this cannot be lost downstream.

Two smaller traps, both handled:

- `gravity` magnitude is unit to within 2e-5 on every trial: per-trial means span
  0.999982–1.000000, with within-trial standard deviations of order 1e-6. That is **not**
  evidence the fusion is tracking — CoreMotion normalises the gravity vector by
  construction. It is a file-corruption check and nothing more.
- Every trial contains handling transients at one or both ends (the participant pocketing
  and retrieving the phone). 417 s across the dataset is trimmed before measurement, using
  rotation-invariant acceleration magnitude so the trim is independent of stage 2. Skipping
  this step corrupts both cadence and the frame estimate.

Measured durations are 15.3–110.7 s. Worth noting only because the short end is shorter
than the 30 s you had in mind: **six** trials are under 20 s (15.3, 15.8, 18.4, 18.6, 19.2,
19.7 s), and 14 are under 25 s.

## 3. Stage 2 — orientation

### Vertical: solid, but only because it tracks

Instantaneous gravity direction wanders **15.1° median, 30.2° at p95, 53° max** away from
the trial-mean direction *within a single trial*. That is the thigh swinging. Consequence:

> **A single trial-constant rotation matrix is invalid on 0/48 trials.**

The default frame mode is therefore `tracking`: `up` is taken from instantaneous gravity at
every sample and the forward axis is re-projected into each sample's horizontal plane. This
is not a pocket-specific hack — the formulation assumes nothing about placement, it just
stops assuming the sensor is rigid with the trunk. Static and tracking vertical
acceleration correlate 0.93–1.00 (median 0.99), so the static frame is not catastrophic for
*amplitude*; it is simply not defensible as a frame.

### Forward: determined, but not verified as forward

Two estimators, both placement-agnostic in formulation:

| estimator | median eigenvalue ratio | well-conditioned (≥4.0) |
|---|---|---|
| broadband PCA (as specified) | 2.11 | **3/48** |
| step-band cross-spectrum (default) | 13.42 | **44/48** |

Broadband horizontal acceleration at the pocket is nearly isotropic — there is no dominant
horizontal axis to find. The step-band estimator restricts to a narrow band around the step
fundamental, on the reasoning that braking and propulsion happen once per step and so
concentrate fore-aft energy there. It is far better conditioned, and the two estimators
disagree by a median of 10.2°.

**But conditioning is not correctness.** The step-band axis is well determined; the claim
that it points *forward* rests on the thigh swinging in the sagittal plane, which is a
pocket-specific argument. This dataset offers no way to confirm it.

The 180° sign is resolved by a braking criterion (fore-aft acceleration should be negative
at the vertical-acceleration peak) and is confident on 35/48. Nothing in stages 3 or 4
depends on it — it sets plot polarity and the left/right naming of the ML axis, not any
number computed.

### Mediolateral: the cross-check fails, and it is a real check

At the trunk, biomechanics predicts a frequency separation: fore-aft acceleration is driven
once per **step**, mediolateral acceleration once per **stride**. So the horizontal axis
carrying most stride-rate power should sit ~90° from the axis carrying most step-rate power.

Measured: the two axes are **20.5° apart on average** (median 16.5°) — nearly parallel, not
perpendicular. The prediction fails on 46/48 trials. At the thigh both harmonics ride on the
same physical axis, the leg's swing plane. The ML axis is left as an unverified construction
of the cross product, and stage 2 says so in its verdict rather than reporting a number.

I expect this check to *start passing* at the sacrum. Keep it as a gate.

### The finding that drives everything downstream

The strongest autocorrelation lag of vertical acceleration is at **two step periods (the
stride), not one, on all 48 trials**. Step-lag regularity ranges 0.03–0.83 while stride-lag
regularity is a tight 0.48–0.92. The step symmetry index (their ratio) has a median of 0.56
and reaches 0.05.

Consecutive steps are not interchangeable. That is exactly what a sensor on **one** thigh
produces: the ipsilateral footstrike and the contralateral one look different. It is the
single most placement-dependent property of this dataset, and it propagates into stages 3
and 4.

(It also means a naive periodicity test at the step lag would fail a perfectly good frame.
The acceptance test is applied at the stride lag; step-lag regularity is reported as a
symmetry index instead.)

## 4. Stage 3 — steps and cadence

### Filter cutoffs and why

Both cutoffs are multiples of the *estimated step frequency*, never fixed Hz, so the filter
is identical in relative terms at any cadence and any sample rate.

- **High-pass 0.7 × f_step.** Sits between the stride subharmonic (0.5 × f_step) and the
  fundamental. A 4th-order Butterworth puts the subharmonic down ~12 dB for 0.25 dB of the
  fundamental. That subharmonic is the step-to-step asymmetry term; leaving it in makes
  alternate peaks systematically shorter and invites missed steps.
- **Low-pass 1.5 × f_step.** Keeps the fundamental, excludes the second harmonic. The
  second harmonic carries a median of **11.6%** of fundamental power here and reaches
  **67%**, and passing it puts a genuine second crest inside every step cycle —
  ensemble-averaging the step cycle gives exactly 2.0 peaks per cycle at a 3 × f_step
  cutoff versus 1.0 at 1.5 ×. Swept against an independent spectral cadence estimate,
  1.5 × gave **0.58%** median error and was the only setting that rejected the harmonic on
  filter shape alone rather than leaning on the peak-spacing rule to hide it. Disabling the
  peak-spacing rule entirely leaves 1.5 × unchanged (×1.0) while 2.0 × degrades by ×2.4 and
  3.0 × by ×30.
- Zero-phase `sosfiltfilt`, so step timestamps carry no group-delay bias; second-order
  sections because a 4th-order `ba` design is numerically unstable at these cutoff/Nyquist
  ratios (0.04 at 50 Hz, 0.01 at 200 Hz).

Peak spacing is 0.6 step periods (allows 1.67× the mean cadence — far more than a runner
varies) and prominence is 0.5 × robust MAD-sigma (accepts the weaker step of an asymmetric
pair; raising it to 1.5 multiplied disagreement with the spectral estimate by **5.0**, from
0.58% to 2.90%, by dropping alternate steps).

### Validation, all 24 subjects

Cadence 148.5–183.7 spm, median 164.7. **47/48 inside 150–190 spm.**

Attribution uses a second estimator that shares no machinery with peak detection — the
spectral peak. The two agree to within **4.11%** on every single trial (ratio 0.961–1.041;
the worst case is `jog_16/sub_13`), so no trial is an algorithm failure.

That attribution has a precondition, since both estimators share `fs_hz`: given a wrong
sample rate they agree with each other while both being wrong. `steps.check_sample_rate`
is therefore consulted first and can return the cause `sample_rate` — see §6.

| flagged trial | detected | spectral | cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| `jog_9/sub_4` | 148.5 spm | 148.9 spm | **trial, not algorithm** — the two independent estimators agree to 0%; this runner's cadence really is just below 150 spm |

Subject 4 is 90 kg / 176 cm and jogs at ~149 spm in trial 9 and ~152 spm in trial 16. That
is a real cadence, marginally under the band, not a detection failure.

Cross-trial reproducibility: a subject's two jog trials give cadences differing by a median
of 2.0 spm (max 7.3 spm).

### One artifact you must not read as a gait metric

Detected step intervals alternate short–long by up to **32%**. Real runners show 1–3%.
The quantity is reported as an absolute value (`alternating_interval_asymmetry_abs_pct`):
its sign is meaningless, because which of the two interleaved interval sets counts as
"even" depends entirely on which step lands at index 0, so a signed version would flip on a
one-step change in the trim boundary while describing identical gait.
This is a **detector timing artifact**, not step-time asymmetry: when the two steps of a
stride produce differently shaped vertical waveforms, the crest of the band-passed
fundamental shifts by a different amount on alternate steps, so the timestamps alternate
even when the runner's true step times do not. The evidence is a correlation of **r =
−0.908** between the magnitude of this interval asymmetry and the amplitude-domain step
symmetry index — one underlying asymmetry driving both. The column is documented as a
diagnostic, not a metric.

Second-order consequence, handled: the cadence smoother must use an **even** window. An
odd-length median sits on one side of a period-2 alternation and sawtooths at exactly the
rate it is meant to suppress. `cadence_series` raises on an odd window rather than quietly
producing that.

## 5. Stage 4 — left/right (exploratory; no ground truth)

**This dataset has no left/right foot labels, and no record of which trouser pocket held
the phone.** No accuracy is claimed anywhere. Labels are `A`/`B`, never left/right.

### Result 1 — as specified, it does not work here

| quantity | median over 48 trials |
|---|---|
| alternation at the detected contact | **0.485** |
| phase-randomised surrogate null | 0.561 |
| random-sign null | 0.500 |
| excess over surrogate | **−0.051** |

Alternation at our best available contact proxy is at chance, and *below* its own null.
Only 3/48 trials reach 0.90; only 13/48 beat the surrogate by more than 0.10.

### Result 2 — the number is dominated by sampling phase

Alternation swings from ~0.09 to ~1.00 across sampling phase **within a single trial**
(median range 0.833). Stage 3 delivers a step-rate phase marker, not a true initial
contact, and the theory names an instant we cannot locate. At the best phase the median
alternation is 0.955 — from the same data that gives 0.485 at the marker.

### Result 3 — there is structure beyond the spectrum, and it recurs within a runner

Compared against a properly constructed null (sweep-and-maximise on each surrogate, so the
selection over ten offsets appears in the null too — comparing a maximum to a mean would be
selection-biased), **40/48 trials exceed the null's 95th percentile at their best phase.**
So ω_v carries something beyond its power spectrum.

That best phase also recurs within a runner **well above chance**. An earlier version of
this report claimed the opposite, on an exact-bin agreement count that ignored the sweep's
own quantisation; the verification pass corrected it. Across a subject's two jog trials:

| agreement between a subject's two trials | observed | chance |
|---|---|---|
| exact sweep bin | **9/24** | 2.4/24 — binomial p = 3.2 × 10⁻⁴ |
| within one bin, circular | **18/24** | 7.2/24 |

The median circular disagreement is **0.10 of a step period — exactly one sweep bin**, which
is the resolution floor of a 10-offset sweep (about 2 samples at 50 Hz). Two genuinely
matching phases that straddle a bin boundary score as a miss under exact equality, which is
why that metric understated the result. `scripts/run_all.py` now prints both tolerances
alongside their chance rates so neither reading can stand alone.

The honest position is therefore narrower than "unusable" and much narrower than "it works".
ω_v does carry step-locked structure, and its phase recurs within a runner. What is missing
is everything that would turn that into a left/right classifier: any label to check it
against, and any reason to think the phase transfers — the best offset scatters across the
whole step cycle *between* subjects, consistent with each phone sitting in a different
pocket at a different angle. Stage 4 stays exploratory because there is no ground truth,
not because there is no signal.

### Why consistency is not correctness — made quantitative

If ω_v carries strong power at the stride rate (half the step rate), then sampling it once
per step lands on alternating half-cycles *whatever its physical origin and whatever its
phase*. A pure sinusoid at f_step/2 sampled every 1/f_step alternates perfectly while
encoding nothing about laterality. Here ω_v has a stride/step power ratio with a median of
2.0, reaching 58. The three controls (`random`, phase-randomised `surrogate`, `mid_step`)
exist to bound exactly that, and the mid-step control frequently *beats* the real contact
sample — on the reference subject, 0.949 versus 0.539.

### Pocket versus sacrum, mechanistically

At the sacrum the theory is testing pelvic transverse rotation: a small, symmetric,
midline signal that genuinely reverses between left and right stance. At the front trouser
pocket the sensor is on **one thigh**. The dominant contribution to ω_v is that leg's own
hip rotation, so what alternates is mostly *ipsilateral swing versus contralateral swing* —
a much larger signal (ω_v RMS measured here: 0.88–3.64 rad/s, median 2.18) with a different
physical origin. It happens to alternate with the correct period, which is precisely why
alternation consistency cannot distinguish it from the effect being sought.

Two further consequences unique to the pocket: the sign convention is tied to *which leg
carries the phone*, and the dataset never recorded which pocket each subject used — so even
if the sign separated the sides perfectly, the mapping from sign to anatomical side is
undetermined per subject. This is not something a better algorithm fixes.

## 6. Where the pocket and 50 Hz are doing load-bearing work

| # | Dependency | Severity at sacrum / 100–200 Hz |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Stage 4 in its entirety.** Depends on contact timing we cannot recover, on a sensor on one thigh, and on a pocket side that was never recorded. | **Rebuild from scratch.** Nothing here transfers. Treat the code as an instrument for asking the question, not as an answer. |
| 2 | **Detected peak is a phase marker, not initial contact.** At 50 Hz the impact transient that marks true contact is above the usable band; the ±10%-of-step contact window is only **5 samples**. | **High.** At 200 Hz the transient is resolvable and true contact becomes detectable — that is a *different algorithm*, not a retuned one. Everything phase-sensitive is blocked until then. |
| 3 | **Low-pass at 1.5 × f_step**, chosen against pocket harmonic content (2nd harmonic median 11.6%, max 67% of fundamental). | **Medium.** Re-run the cutoff sweep at the sacrum; harmonic structure will differ. At 200 Hz this band also discards the impact transient you will want for loading rate later. |
| 4 | **Forward axis via step-band cross-spectrum.** Beats broadband PCA 13.4 : 2.1 on conditioning *at the pocket*. | **Medium, benign.** Fore-aft should dominate more at the sacrum, so plain PCA may become viable. Re-measure the eigenvalue ratio; do not assume either way. |
| 5 | **Tracking frame mode.** Exists because the thigh swings 15–30°. | **Low.** Sacrum tilt is far smaller, so a static frame may become valid; tracking still works and costs nothing. Safe either way. |
| 6 | **Stride-lag periodicity acceptance** rather than step-lag, forced by pocket step asymmetry (symmetry index down to 0.05). | **Low, and correct anyway.** The stride is gait's true repeat period regardless of placement. |
| 7 | **Steady-state trim threshold** (0.5 × median 1 s RMS) calibrated on pocket signal amplitudes. | **Low.** The gap between running and handling is wide; re-check but expect it to hold. |

Explicitly **not** load-bearing, and measured rather than asserted:

- **Orientation.** Rotating every sensor vector by an arbitrary rotation changes cadence by
  **0.00 spm** and preserves step count in **144/144** rotated runs across all 48 trials.
  Exact, not approximate.
- **Sample rate.** Resampled to 25 / 100 / 200 Hz, cadence moves by a median of
  0.034–0.049% and at worst 0.787%. The number 50 appears once in the package
  (`loader.DEFAULT_FS_HZ`) and only as a default argument.
- One genuine rate cliff was found and now fails loudly: clamping the low-pass to the
  Nyquist cap could push the step fundamental out of its own passband while `low < high`
  still held, producing a filter that removed the component being detected and reporting no
  error. It binds when `fs < 2.5 × f_step` and now raises. (A narrower silent clamp remains
  between `2.5 × f_step` and `3.75 × f_step`, where the low-pass is pinned to `0.4 × fs`
  rather than `1.5 × f_step`; it cannot be reached at running cadence and is pinned by an
  xfail test.)
- `fs_hz` is an input assertion, and getting it wrong rescales every result linearly while
  both cadence estimators keep agreeing with each other. `steps.check_sample_rate` now tests
  the claimed rate against the data — comparing the strongest spectral peak inside the
  plausible human step band against the strongest peak outside it — and
  `diagnose_cadence` reports the cause `sample_rate` rather than blaming the runner. Across
  all 48 trials at the true rate the ratio never exceeds 0.435; presenting 50 Hz data as
  100 Hz gives 15.1 and as 200 Hz gives 1002. **Blind spot, stated rather than papered
  over: understating the rate by ~2× is undetectable**, because it moves a 2.7 Hz
  fundamental to 1.36 Hz, still inside the human band and indistinguishable from a slow
  walker.

## 7. What this dataset fundamentally cannot tell you

1. **Anything requiring true initial contact.** Contact time, loading rate, braking
   impulse, vertical oscillation, flight time. 50 Hz on a soft-tissue-coupled pocket does
   not resolve the impact transient. (Not built, per your instructions — this is *why* that
   was the right call.)
2. **Whether left/right discrimination works.** No labels exist. The best this data
   supports is what is reported: alternation consistency, its null distributions, and the
   observation that the result is dominated by an unrecoverable phase offset.
3. **Whether the forward axis is really forward.** No motion-capture reference, no heading
   ground truth. The axis is well determined and its anatomical identity is an inference.
4. **Anything about the sacrum.** All 48 trials are pocket. Every placement-related number
   here is a pocket number. The cross-checks are the transferable artifact, not the values.
5. **Sampling integrity.** No timestamps. Dropped samples, jitter and drift are invisible,
   so no claim about timing robustness can be validated here.
6. **Within-runner change over time.** One session per subject, no repeated days. Pace
   normalisation and changepoint detection are unbuildable — as you said. (Not built.)
7. **Anything at pace variation.** Trials are single-bout steady jogging, 15–111 s. There
   is no pace ladder, no fatigue arc, no terrain change.
8. **Subject 4's cadence is a data point, not a bug.** Worth keeping in mind that a 150 spm
   floor will flag genuine runners.
