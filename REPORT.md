# Gait signal core — findings

Scope: **48 jog trials** (24 subjects × trials 9 and 16), MotionSense `A_DeviceMotion_data`,
50 Hz nominal, iPhone 6s in the front trouser pocket — plus, since the September 2026 audit,
synthetic sessions in the exact format the **BaselineLogger** iOS app writes, so the two
halves of the system are proven to connect.

**Provenance — what is traceable and what is not.** Most numbers below are per-trial columns
in `reports/pipeline_results.csv` and `reports/invariance.csv`, regenerated by
`scripts/run_all.py` and `scripts/run_invariance_checks.py`: cadence and its diagnosis, the
stage-2 verdicts and both forward-sign criteria, tilt, step and stride regularity, the
symmetry index, steady-state fragmentation, alternation and its null models, the quality
roll-up, and both invariance sweeps. Re-running those two scripts reproduces every one of
them. Four groups are **not** in either CSV and come from one-off analyses over the same
trials: the broadband-PCA vs step-band conditioning comparison (§3), the second-harmonic
power ratio (§4), the static-vs-tracking vertical correlation (§3), and the low-pass cutoff
sweep (§4). The harmonic-rejection claim is pinned by
`tests/test_failure_modes.py::test_lowpass_rejects_the_second_harmonic_on_filter_shape_alone`;
the rest are reproducible only by hand. Treat them as measured but not continuously verified.

## 2. Stage 1 — loader and explorer

Layout was read from the dataset's own README and verified on disk before any loader code
was written: `A_DeviceMotion_data/<activity>_<trial>/sub_<subject>.csv`, jog = trials 9 and
16, 24 subjects each, 12 signal columns plus an unnamed integer row counter.

**MotionSense has no timestamp column.** Its time index is `arange(n) / fs_hz` — a grid we
impose — and dropped samples, jitter and drift are undetectable. Every MotionSense integrity
record carries `has_timestamp_column: False` and `timestamp_irregularities_detectable: False`.

**BaselineLogger sessions do have timestamps, and the loader uses them.** `t` is a per-sample
hardware timestamp on the monotonic clock, so for the app's own data the sample rate is
measured (median interval), jitter is measured (robust spread, sub-millisecond on real
hardware), and every irregularity is counted independently of `session.json`:

- gaps (> 3× median, the app's own rule),
- samples dropped below the gap threshold (> 1.5× median; one dropped sample is 2×, which the
  gap rule never sees),
- duplicated or reordered samples (delta ≤ 0),
- an unfinished session (`inProgress` in `session.json`),
- the app's sample count against the rows actually in the file.

A gapped session is **refused by default** (`on_gap="raise"`), because every filter downstream
assumes a uniform grid and a uniform index laid over a gap puts every later timestamp in the
wrong place. `on_gap="longest"` keeps the longest clean stretch and reports what was cut, and
a cut record is never `clean`. GPS is loaded separately with the cached pre-session fix,
invalid speeds and invalid accuracies dropped and counted.

Two smaller traps on MotionSense, both handled: `gravity` magnitude is unit to 2e-5 by
CoreMotion's construction (a corruption check, not evidence the fusion tracks), and every
trial contains handling transients at one or both ends — 88 s across the dataset is trimmed,
not the 417 s previously claimed, which was mostly discarded running (fix #2).

Measured durations are 15.3–110.7 s; six trials are under 20 s.

## 3. Stage 2 — orientation

### Vertical: solid, but only because it tracks

Instantaneous gravity direction wanders **15.4° median, 30.9° at p95, 53° max** from the
trial-mean direction within a single trial — the thigh swinging. A single trial-constant
rotation is invalid on **0/48** trials. The default frame mode is therefore `tracking`: `up`
from instantaneous gravity at every sample, forward re-projected into each sample's horizontal
plane. Static and tracking vertical acceleration correlate 0.93–1.00, so the static frame is
not catastrophic for amplitude; it is simply not defensible as a frame.

**Against ground truth.** On synthetic body-frame signals rotated by arbitrary rotations,
including upside-down and 180° yaw, `build_frame` recovers `up` to < 0.5° and `forward` to
< 3° with the correct sign, and the resolved vertical channel equals the true one to 1e-9.

### Forward: determined; anatomically forward only where shown

| estimator | median eigenvalue ratio | well-conditioned (≥ 4.0) |
|---|---|---|
| broadband PCA (as specified) | 2.11 | **3/48** |
| step-band cross-spectrum (default) | 13.42 | **45/48** |

Broadband horizontal acceleration at the pocket is nearly isotropic. The step-band estimator
restricts to a narrow band around the step fundamental (braking and propulsion happen once
per step) and is far better conditioned; the two disagree by a median of 10°.

**The 180° sign — audit fix #3.** Two criteria, both from the same stance-phase biomechanics:

- **phase** (primary): braking coincides with *rising* vertical acceleration, so
  `corr(a_fwd, d a_vert/dt) < 0`. This is the relation at the centre of mass — the lower back —
  where the vertical peak is midstance and fore-aft force crosses zero.
- **impact** (the original criterion, now a cross-check): fore-aft negative *at* the vertical
  peak. Valid where the peak is early stance (a thigh); at the centre of mass it samples the
  zero crossing.

On pocket data they have equal effect size (median |stat| 0.20 and 0.19) and equal split-half
consistency, yet **disagree on the sign in 18/48 trials**. Requiring both to agree gives
**confident 18/48**, down from the 35/48 the old single-criterion definition reported.
Internal consistency was never evidence of correctness. Nothing in stages 3 or 4 depends on
the sign; it sets plot polarity and the left/right naming of the ML axis.

### Mediolateral: the cross-check fails, and it is a real check

At the trunk, fore-aft acceleration is driven once per **step**, mediolateral once per
**stride**, so the two horizontal power axes should sit ~90° apart. Measured: **19.6° mean**
(median 15.7°) — nearly parallel. The prediction fails on 46/48. On the synthetic sacrum-like
signal the same check passes at > 80°, which is what it should do; keep it as a gate.

### The finding that drives everything downstream

The strongest autocorrelation lag of vertical acceleration is at **two step periods (the
stride), not one, on all 48 trials**. Step-lag regularity 0.03–0.83; stride-lag 0.48–0.91.
Symmetry index median 0.56, minimum 0.04. Consecutive steps are not interchangeable — a
sensor on **one** thigh. The acceptance test is applied at the stride lag; step-lag
regularity is reported as a symmetry index.

## 4. Stage 3 — running-state, steps, cadence

### Running-state detection — audit fix #2

Acceleration magnitude (rotation-invariant) in 1 s windows; a window is running when its RMS
is ≥ 0.5× the trial median. **Sub-threshold dips shorter than 3 s are bridged**: longer than
any single-step anomaly (~0.35 s) or a turn (1–2 s), far shorter than a real stop. Without
this, 14/48 continuous trials split on a single low second and lost half their length. A real
stop still splits the run; every bout is reported, the longest is analysed, and the seconds
outside it are a column. The product's multi-bout policy (per-bout vs pooled) is a design
decision not made here.

### Filter cutoffs and why

Both cutoffs are multiples of the estimated step frequency, so the filter is identical in
relative terms at any cadence and any rate (the one silent exception, a clamp between
2.5× and 3.75× f_step, cannot be reached at running cadence and is pinned by an xfail test).

- **High-pass 0.7 × f_step**: between the stride subharmonic and the fundamental; ~12 dB on
  the subharmonic for 0.25 dB on the fundamental.
- **Low-pass 1.5 × f_step**: the second harmonic carries 11.6 % of fundamental power at the
  median and 67 % at most, and passing it puts a second crest in every step cycle (2.0
  peaks/cycle at 3× vs 1.0 at 1.5×). With the peak-spacing rule disabled, 1.5× is unchanged
  while 2.0× degrades ×2.4 and 3.0× by ×30: the filter, not the spacing rule, does the work.
- Zero-phase `sosfiltfilt`; second-order sections.

Peak spacing 0.6 step periods; prominence 0.5 × robust MAD-sigma, with a zero or near-zero
sigma treated as **no signal** (a constant input used to yield 47 "steps"). Raising prominence
to 1.5 multiplied disagreement with the spectral estimate by 5.

### Validation, all 24 subjects

Cadence **148.5–181.2 spm**, median 163.3. **47/48 inside 150–190 spm.** The two independent
estimators agree within **5.9 %** on every trial (ratio 0.941–1.041). The worst cases are
bridged trials (`jog_16/sub_10`, `jog_16/sub_17`), where the previously discarded second now
enters the detector; their interval irregularity is correspondingly higher (2.7 % vs 1.7 %
median for unbridged trials), which is the honest cost of analysing the whole trial.

| flagged | detected | spectral | cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| `jog_9/sub_4` | 148.5 spm | 148.9 spm | **trial** — the estimators agree to 0 %; this runner really is just under 150 |

Attribution is four-way: `none` / `sample_rate` / `algorithm` / `trial`. The sample-rate check
runs first because both estimators share `fs_hz` and would agree with each other under a
wrong rate; across all 48 trials at the true rate its out-of-band/in-band power ratio never
exceeds 0.44, while 50 Hz data presented as 100 Hz gives 15 and as 200 Hz gives 1000.
**Understating the rate by ~2× remains undetectable** (2.7 Hz becomes 1.36 Hz, inside the
human band). For logger sessions the rate is measured, so the check guards only against a
corrupted `t` column.

A subject's two jog trials give cadences differing by a median of 2.1 spm (max 8.2).

### One artifact you must not read as a gait metric

Detected step intervals alternate short–long by up to **34 %**; real runners show 1–3 %. It is
a detector timing artifact — different waveform shapes on alternate steps shift the crest of
the band-passed fundamental — and it correlates at **r = −0.77** with the amplitude-domain
symmetry index. Reported as an absolute value, since its sign depends on which step lands at
index 0. The cadence smoother uses an even window for the same reason.

## 5. Stage 4 — left/right (exploratory; no ground truth)

**No left/right labels exist, and the dataset never recorded which pocket held the phone.**
Labels are `A`/`B`. No accuracy is claimed. The quality roll-up reports side classification as
information and never gates on it.

### Result 1 — as specified, it does not work here

| quantity | median over 48 trials |
|---|---|
| alternation at the detected contact | **0.486** |
| phase-randomised surrogate null | 0.551 |
| excess over surrogate | **−0.027** |

Only 3/48 reach 0.90; only 12/48 beat the surrogate by more than 0.10.

### Result 2 — the number is dominated by sampling phase

Alternation swings from ~0.1 to ~1.0 across sampling phase within a trial (median range
0.80). Stage 3 delivers a phase marker, not initial contact. Best-phase median 0.945.

### Result 3 — there is structure beyond the spectrum, and it recurs within a runner

Against a sweep-and-maximise surrogate null (so the selection over ten offsets appears in the
null too), **43/48 trials exceed the null's 95th percentile at their best phase.** The best
phase recurs within a runner above chance: exact-bin agreement **8/24** (chance 2.4, binomial
p = 1.7 × 10⁻³), within one circular bin **20/24** (chance 7.2), median disagreement one bin —
the sweep's resolution floor. What is missing is any label to check it against and any
reason to think the phase transfers between runners or placements. Stage 4 stays exploratory
because there is no ground truth, not because there is no signal.

**On the synthetic sacrum-like session the side classification reports `unreliable`**, and
that is correct: a pure stride-rate sinusoid alternates perfectly by construction and so does
its surrogate — no information beyond the spectrum, which is exactly what the null exists to
expose.

### Pocket versus sacrum, mechanically

At the sacrum the theory tests pelvic transverse rotation, a small midline signal. At the
pocket the sensor is on one thigh and ω_v is dominated by that leg's own hip rotation (RMS
0.88–3.65 rad/s, median 2.25; stride/step power ratio median 1.7, up to 58). It alternates with
the right period for the wrong reason, which is why alternation consistency cannot distinguish
it from the effect sought. The sign is also tied to which leg carries the phone, which was
never recorded.

## 6. Where placement and rate are doing load-bearing work

| # | Dependency | At sacrum / 100–200 Hz |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Stage 4 entirely.** Contact timing we cannot recover; a sensor on one thigh; a pocket side never recorded. | **Rebuild from scratch.** |
| 2 | **Detected peak is a phase marker, not initial contact.** At 50 Hz the contact window is 5 samples. | **High.** At 200 Hz the transient is resolvable and true contact becomes detectable — a different algorithm, not a retune. |
| 3 | **Low-pass at 1.5 × f_step**, chosen against pocket harmonic content. | **Medium.** Re-run the sweep at the sacrum; at 200 Hz this band also discards the impact transient wanted for loading rate. |
| 4 | **Forward via step-band cross-spectrum**; sign via the phase criterion. | **Medium.** The phase criterion is *designed* for the centre of mass and proven only on synthetic data there; the step-band estimator's conditioning is a pocket measurement. Re-measure both. |
| 5 | **Tracking frame mode**, because the thigh swings 15–30°. | **Low.** Sacrum tilt is far smaller; tracking still works. |
| 6 | **Stride-lag acceptance**, forced by pocket step asymmetry. | **Low, and correct anyway.** |
| 7 | **Steady-state trim** (0.5 × median RMS, 3 s bridge). | **Low.** Re-check the bridge length against real stops; 3 s is a judgement. |

Explicitly **not** load-bearing, and measured:

- **Orientation.** Arbitrary rotations of every sensor vector change cadence by **0.00 spm**
  and preserve step count in **144/144** rotated runs. Exact.
- **Sample rate.** Resampled to 25 / 100 / 200 Hz, cadence moves by a median of 0.03 % and at
  worst **0.24 % (100 Hz), 0.37 % (200 Hz), 1.8 % (25 Hz — a bridged trial at half the native
  rate, a genuine information-loss case)**. The number 50 appears once in the package and only
  as a default. Logger sessions never use it: the rate is measured.

## 7. What this data fundamentally cannot tell you

1. **Anything requiring true initial contact** — contact time, loading rate, braking impulse,
   vertical oscillation. Not built; this is why.
2. **Whether left/right discrimination works.** No labels.
3. **Whether the forward axis is really forward on a runner.** Proven on synthetic signals;
   no motion-capture reference for real ones.
4. **Anything about the sacrum from MotionSense.** All 48 trials are pocket. The cross-checks
   are the transferable artifact, not the values.
5. **Sampling integrity of MotionSense.** No timestamps. (Logger sessions: now measurable.)
6. **Within-runner change over time.** One session per subject.
7. **Anything at pace variation.** Single steady bouts, 15–111 s. `gps.csv` now carries speed
   with its accuracy, but nothing consumes it yet.
8. **Subject 4's cadence is a data point, not a bug.** A 150 spm floor will flag real runners.

## 8. Not built, per instructions

No vertical oscillation, braking impulse, loading rate, ground contact time, or asymmetry
(they need true initial contact and the sample rate MotionSense lacks). No pace model,
residuals, within-run trajectory, or changepoint detection (they need multi-session per-runner
data at known pace; the logger now records what they would need). No report UI. The audit
brief describes these as intended product features; they have no code in either repository,
and this report does not audit what does not exist.

## 9. How the audit was done, and its limits

- **Python:** every finding was reproduced with code before being fixed; every fix has a
  regression test (102 tests, 2 deliberate xfails). All 48 trials and the notebook run with
  zero warnings.
- **Swift:** **no Swift toolchain was available.** Every iOS change was made by static review,
  the tracker arithmetic was validated by a line-for-line Python port (gaps, dropped samples,
  duplicates, reversals, a 60 Hz device, a mid-session rate change), and symbol/call-site
  consistency was checked by hand. The changes have not been compiled or run on a device.
  Build in Xcode and do a locked-screen test run before trusting a long session.
- **Adversarial fan-out:** six independent reviewer lenses with two refuters per finding were
  launched. The two Swift reviewers completed (15 findings, every one checked against the
  source by hand; the substantive ones are fixes #5–#8 above). The four Python reviewers and
  all verifiers hit a subagent session limit on the first run; a resumed run was still in
  progress when this report was written and its findings, if any, are not reflected here.
- **Unverified claims that remain:** whether When-In-Use location authorization alone keeps
  capture alive after screen lock on iOS 16 (the app now says so with a caveat and asks for a
  test run); whether any iPhone delivers 200 Hz through `CMMotionManager`; the 3 s bridge
  length against real stops.
