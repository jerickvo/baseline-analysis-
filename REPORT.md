# Gait signal core — findings

Scope: **48 jog trials** (24 subjects × trials 9 and 16), MotionSense `A_DeviceMotion_data`,
50 Hz nominal, iPhone 6s in the front trouser pocket.

**Provenance — what is traceable and what is not.** Every number in sections 1–6 that
describes a per-trial quantity is a column in `reports/pipeline_results.csv` or
`reports/invariance.csv`, regenerated by `scripts/run_all.py` and
`scripts/run_invariance_checks.py`. The audit re-ran both scripts from a clean checkout and
the committed CSVs and figures came back **byte-identical**; after the audit's fixes they
were regenerated again and the numbers below are from that run.

Three groups of numbers are **not** in either CSV, because the quantities are not among the
columns `pipeline.flatten` emits:

- the broadband-PCA vs step-band conditioning comparison (§3),
- the static-vs-tracking vertical correlation (§3),
- the low-pass cutoff / prominence sweep and the ensemble peaks-per-cycle figure (§4).

These come from one-off analyses over the same 48 trials. The audit re-derived all three
under the current code; the conditioning table, the correlation range and the sweep
reproduced to within rounding, and the ensemble figure did **not** — see §4, where the
claim is corrected. The harmonic-rejection claim is pinned by
`tests/test_failure_modes.py::test_lowpass_rejects_the_second_harmonic_on_filter_shape_alone`.
The second-harmonic power ratio, previously in this list, is now a CSV column
(`second_harmonic_ratio`). Treat the remaining three as measured but not continuously
verified.

## 2. Stage 1 — loader and explorer

Layout was read from the dataset's own README and then verified on disk before any loader
code was written: `A_DeviceMotion_data/<activity>_<trial>/sub_<subject>.csv`, jog = trials
9 and 16, 24 subjects each, 12 signal columns plus an unnamed integer row counter.

**The one irregularity that matters is the one that cannot be measured.** There is no
timestamp column anywhere in this dataset. The time index is `arange(n) / fs_hz` — a grid
we impose. Dropped samples, clock jitter and rate drift are all undetectable. A 97.2 s
trial and a 100 s trial that lost 140 samples are byte-identical to this loader. Every
integrity record carries `has_timestamp_column: False` and
`timestamp_irregularities_detectable: False` so this cannot be lost downstream.

Two smaller traps, both handled:

- `gravity` magnitude is unit to within 2e-5 on every trial: per-trial means span
  0.999982–1.000000, with within-trial standard deviations of order 1e-6. That is **not**
  evidence the fusion is tracking — CoreMotion normalises the gravity vector by
  construction. It is a file-corruption check and nothing more.
- Every trial contains handling transients at one or both ends (the participant pocketing
  and retrieving the phone). 417 s across the dataset is trimmed before measurement, using
  rotation-invariant acceleration magnitude so the trim is independent of stage 2. Skipping
  this step corrupts both cadence and the frame estimate.

Measured durations are 15.3–110.7 s. Worth noting only because the short end is shorter
than the 30 s you had in mind: **six** trials are under 20 s (15.3, 15.8, 18.4, 18.6, 19.2,
19.7 s), and 14 are under 25 s.

## 3. Stage 2 — orientation

### Vertical: solid, but only because it tracks

Instantaneous gravity direction wanders **15.1° median, 30.2° at p95, 53° max** away from
the trial-mean direction *within a single trial*. That is the thigh swinging. Consequence:

> **A single trial-constant rotation matrix is invalid on 0/48 trials.**

The default frame mode is therefore `tracking`: `up` is taken from instantaneous gravity at
every sample and the forward axis is re-projected into each sample's horizontal plane. This
is not a pocket-specific hack — the formulation assumes nothing about placement, it just
stops assuming the sensor is rigid with the trunk. Static and tracking vertical
acceleration correlate 0.93–1.00 (median 0.99), so the static frame is not catastrophic for
*amplitude*; it is simply not defensible as a frame.

### Forward: determined, but not verified as forward

Two estimators, both placement-agnostic in formulation:

| estimator | median eigenvalue ratio | well-conditioned (≥4.0) |
|---|---|---|
| broadband PCA (as specified) | 2.11 | **3/48** |
| step-band cross-spectrum (default) | 14.20 | **43/48** |

Broadband horizontal acceleration at the pocket is nearly isotropic — there is no dominant
horizontal axis to find. The step-band estimator restricts to a narrow band around the step
fundamental, on the reasoning that braking and propulsion happen once per step and so
concentrate fore-aft energy there. It is far better conditioned, and the two estimators
disagree by a median of 10.2°.

**But conditioning is not correctness.** The step-band axis is well determined; the claim
that it points *forward* rests on the thigh swinging in the sagittal plane, which is a
pocket-specific argument. This dataset offers no way to confirm it.

**And a determined axis is not a fixed one.** Re-estimated on 8 s windows, the forward axis
wanders from the trial-level axis by a median of 15° at p95 and up to 86°; it holds within
the 10° crosstalk bound on only **16/48** trials. That drift was measured but never entered
the verdict before the audit; a frame is now `ok` only if the forward axis is dominant *and*
stable. The step frequency the axis is built at is also now measured on the same tracking
vertical the rest of the pipeline uses — one trial (`jog_16/sub_1`) had built its forward
axis in a band centred on the 1.5 × f harmonic because the static projection's spectral peak
sat there, and nothing reported it.

The 180° sign is resolved by a braking criterion (fore-aft acceleration should be negative
at the vertical-acceleration peak) and is confident on 35/48. Nothing in stages 3 or 4
depends on it — it sets plot polarity and the left/right naming of the ML axis, not any
number computed.

### Mediolateral: the cross-check fails, and it is a real check

At the trunk, biomechanics predicts a frequency separation: fore-aft acceleration is driven
once per **step**, mediolateral acceleration once per **stride**. So the horizontal axis
carrying most stride-rate power should sit ~90° from the axis carrying most step-rate power.

Two things have to hold for the check to pass, and before the audit only one was tested.
The stride-rate axis must itself be *determined* (eigenvalue ratio ≥ 4): an undetermined
axis points somewhere random and lands 60° or more from the step axis about a third of the
time. This dataset's two earlier `ok` verdicts (`jog_9/sub_19`, `jog_16/sub_19`) were
exactly that — stride-axis ratios 2.05 and 2.49 — while all 41 trials with a determined
stride axis failed. The check is also now computed on the per-sample tracking-resolved
horizontal channels rather than the trial-mean plane: on a synthetic sensor pitching ±20°
at stride rate the mean-plane version collapsed the angle from 90° to 48° on a frame whose
tracking resolution was exact, which is squarely the tilt range this dataset shows.

Measured on the resolved channels: the two axes are **27.5° apart on average (median
26.4°)** — closer to parallel than perpendicular. The check is **unsupported on 44/48**,
**undetermined on 3/48**, and supported on **1/48** (`jog_16/sub_23`, 71°, stride-axis ratio
23) — a trial whose forward axis then fails the stability gate. At the thigh both harmonics
ride on the same physical axis, the leg's swing plane. The ML axis is left as an unverified
construction of the cross product, and stage 2 says so in its verdict rather than reporting
a number.

I expect this check to *start passing* at the sacrum. Keep it as a gate.

### The finding that drives everything downstream

The strongest autocorrelation lag of vertical acceleration is at **two step periods (the
stride), not one, on all 48 trials**. Step-lag regularity ranges 0.03–0.81 while stride-lag
regularity is a tight 0.65–0.93. The step symmetry index (their ratio) has a median of 0.52
and ranges 0.04–0.95. (Two audit fixes moved these: the band the autocorrelation is taken
on now starts at 0.3 × f_step rather than 0.5 ×, so the stride subharmonic is no longer
sitting on the filter corner at −6 dB — that had biased the index upward by +0.04 median
and +0.12 max, and deflated stride regularity to 0.48 on the most asymmetric runner,
`jog_16/sub_13`, now 0.76; and the autocorrelation is read at the exact fractional lag
rather than the nearest sample, which had pushed the index above 1 on two trials.)

Consecutive steps are not interchangeable. That is exactly what a sensor on **one** thigh
produces: the ipsilateral footstrike and the contralateral one look different. It is the
single most placement-dependent property of this dataset, and it propagates into stages 3
and 4.

(It also means a naive periodicity test at the step lag would fail a perfectly good frame.
The acceptance test is applied at the stride lag; step-lag regularity is reported as a
symmetry index instead.)

## 4. Stage 3 — steps and cadence

### Filter cutoffs and why

Both cutoffs are multiples of the *estimated step frequency*, never fixed Hz, so the filter
is identical in relative terms at any cadence and any sample rate.

- **High-pass 0.7 × f_step.** Sits between the stride subharmonic (0.5 × f_step) and the
  fundamental. The order-4 Butterworth band-pass puts the subharmonic down **24 dB per
  pass** (48 dB after the zero-phase double pass) for under 0.05 dB of the fundamental,
  measured with `sosfreqz` — an earlier version of this report said 12 dB / 0.25 dB, which
  understated it by 2×. That subharmonic is the step-to-step asymmetry term; leaving it in
  makes alternate peaks systematically shorter and invites missed steps.
- **Low-pass 1.5 × f_step.** Keeps the fundamental, excludes the second harmonic (22 dB per
  pass). The second harmonic carries a median of **11.4%** of fundamental power here and
  reaches **67%**. Passing it *can* put a second crest inside the step cycle: a drift-free
  ensemble average of the step cycle shows two crests on 3/48 trials at a 3 × f_step cutoff
  and on none at 1.5 ×. (An earlier version claimed "exactly 2.0 peaks per cycle at 3 ×";
  that did not reproduce — the median is 1.0 at both cutoffs — and the method behind it was
  never recorded.) The evidence that actually carries the choice is the sweep: against the
  spectral cadence estimate, 1.5 × gives **0.55%** median error and is the only setting that
  rejects the harmonic on filter shape alone rather than leaning on the peak-spacing rule to
  hide it. Disabling that rule leaves 1.5 × unchanged (×1.0) while 2.0 × degrades by ×2.4
  and 3.0 × by ×33.
- Zero-phase `sosfiltfilt`, so step timestamps carry no group-delay bias; second-order
  sections because a 4th-order `ba` design is numerically unstable at these cutoff/Nyquist
  ratios (0.04 at 50 Hz, 0.01 at 200 Hz).

Peak spacing is 0.6 step periods (allows 1.67× the mean cadence — far more than a runner
varies) and prominence is 0.5 × robust MAD-sigma (accepts the weaker step of an asymmetric
pair; raising it to 1.5 multiplied disagreement with the spectral estimate by **5.2**, from
0.55% to 2.91%, by dropping alternate steps).

### Validation, all 24 subjects

Cadence 148.5–183.7 spm, median 164.7. **47/48 inside 150–190 spm.**

Attribution compares the peak count against the spectral peak. The two agree to within
**3.9%** on every trial (ratio 0.961–1.017; worst case `jog_16/sub_21`).

**What that agreement does and does not show.** An earlier version of this report called
the spectral peak "a second estimator that shares no machinery with peak detection". That
is not true: the detector's band-pass is [0.7, 1.5] × the *spectral* f_step and its minimum
peak spacing is 0.6 of that period, so the detector can only ever count peaks near the
spectral rate. Band-limited noise with no steps in it passes the 10% agreement test on 23 of
30 runs. Agreement therefore rules out *within-band* miscounting — missed or doubled steps
that survive the filter — and nothing more. It is a consistency check, not independent
confirmation, and the "half" / "double" diagnoses in `diagnose_cadence` are defensive: with
this band the ratio stays inside 0.86–1.11 even on noise.

Three ways the two can agree while both being wrong are now handled explicitly, in order:

1. a wrong `fs_hz` — both rescale together; `steps.check_sample_rate` is consulted first
   and can return the cause `sample_rate` (§6);
2. a hole in the detected steps — a 60 s walk break inside a 5 min run dragged the
   whole-span cadence from 168 to 135 spm while the spectrum ignored it, and the resulting
   disagreement was attributed to the *algorithm*. An interval longer than 5 step periods
   (no runner stops stepping that long while running; two missed steps are ~3×, and the
   largest interval on any real trial is 3.4×) is now reported as `detection_gap`, cause
   `trial`. Fires on 0/48 here;
3. a spectral peak on the stride subharmonic — a runner whose stride-rate power exceeds
   step-rate power makes both estimators halve together, and the result used to read "the
   runner's cadence really is 85 spm". When the cadence is below the band, doubling it would
   land inside, and power at twice the detected frequency is ≥ 0.25 of the peak, the cause
   is now `ambiguous`. Cannot fire here (stride/step power never exceeds 0.09) — it exists
   for the placement where it can.

| flagged trial | detected | spectral | cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| `jog_9/sub_4` | 148.5 spm | 148.9 spm | **trial, not algorithm** — the two independent estimators agree to 0%; this runner's cadence really is just below 150 spm |

Subject 4 is 90 kg / 176 cm and jogs at ~149 spm in trial 9 and ~152 spm in trial 16. That
is a real cadence, marginally under the band, not a detection failure.

Cross-trial reproducibility: a subject's two jog trials give cadences differing by a median
of 2.0 spm (max 7.3 spm).

### One artifact you must not read as a gait metric

Detected step intervals alternate short–long by up to **32%**. Real runners show 1–3%.
The quantity is reported as an absolute value (`alternating_interval_asymmetry_abs_pct`):
its sign is meaningless, because which of the two interleaved interval sets counts as
"even" depends entirely on which step lands at index 0, so a signed version would flip on a
one-step change in the trim boundary while describing identical gait.
This is a **detector timing artifact**, not step-time asymmetry: when the two steps of a
stride produce differently shaped vertical waveforms, the crest of the band-passed
fundamental shifts by a different amount on alternate steps, so the timestamps alternate
even when the runner's true step times do not. The evidence is a correlation of **r =
−0.905** between the magnitude of this interval asymmetry and the amplitude-domain step
symmetry index — one underlying asymmetry driving both. The column is documented as a
diagnostic, not a metric.

Second-order consequence, handled: the cadence smoother must use an **even** window. An
odd-length median sits on one side of a period-2 alternation and sawtooths at exactly the
rate it is meant to suppress. `cadence_series` raises on an odd window rather than quietly
producing that.

## 5. Stage 4 — left/right (exploratory; no ground truth)

**This dataset has no left/right foot labels, and no record of which trouser pocket held
the phone.** No accuracy is claimed anywhere. Labels are `A`/`B`, never left/right.

### Result 1 — as specified, it does not work here

| quantity | median over 48 trials |
|---|---|
| alternation at the detected contact | **0.485** |
| phase-randomised surrogate null | 0.561 |
| random-sign null | 0.500 |
| excess over surrogate | **−0.051** |

Alternation at our best available contact proxy is at chance, and *below* its own null.
Only 3/48 trials reach 0.90; only 12/48 beat the surrogate by more than 0.10.

### Result 2 — the number is dominated by sampling phase

Alternation swings from ~0.09 to ~1.00 across sampling phase **within a single trial**
(median range 0.838). Stage 3 delivers a step-rate phase marker, not a true initial
contact, and the theory names an instant we cannot locate. At the best phase the median
alternation is 0.955 — from the same data that gives 0.485 at the marker.

### Result 3 — the best phase recurs within a runner; "beats the null" shows less than it seems

**The null is not the null it was taken for.** Compared against a sweep-and-maximise null
(so the selection over ten offsets appears in the null too), **41/48 trials exceed the
null's 95th percentile at their best phase**, and an earlier version of this report read
that as "ω_v carries something beyond its power spectrum". The audit tested that reading
directly: a pure stride-rate sinusoid locked to the step markers, with noise, and with **no
laterality information by construction**, beats the same p95 on **22 of 24** synthetic runs
(1.2 expected if the null were calibrated). Phase randomisation preserves the spectrum but
destroys the step-lock, and a step-locked stride-rate oscillation — which a sensor on one
swinging leg produces automatically — alternates more than any random-phase surrogate of it.
So "beats the null" certifies step-locked periodicity, which was never in doubt, and says
nothing about laterality. `tests/test_audit_regressions.py::test_phase_randomised_null_does_not_preserve_step_locking`
pins this so the line in `run_all`'s summary cannot be read as a result again.

**What does survive is within-runner recurrence.** The best sampling phase recurs across a
subject's two jog trials well above chance. An earlier version claimed the opposite, on an
exact-bin agreement count that ignored the sweep's own quantisation:

| agreement between a subject's two trials | observed | chance |
|---|---|---|
| exact sweep bin | **9/24** | 2.4/24 — binomial p = 3.2 × 10⁻⁴ |
| within one bin, circular | **19/24** | 7.2/24 — binomial p = 9e-07 |

The median circular disagreement is **0.10 of a step period — exactly one sweep bin**, the
resolution floor of a 10-offset sweep (about 2 samples at 50 Hz). Two genuinely matching
phases that straddle a bin boundary score as a miss under exact equality, which is why that
metric understated the result. `scripts/run_all.py` prints both tolerances alongside their
chance rates so neither reading can stand alone.

The honest position: ω_v's alternation has a preferred phase, and that phase is a stable
property of a given phone in a given pocket on a given runner. Nothing here shows it is a
property of *gait*. What is missing is everything that would turn it into a left/right
classifier: any label to check against, a null that a step-locked but lateral-blind signal
fails, and any reason to think the phase transfers — the best offset scatters across the
whole step cycle *between* subjects, consistent with each phone sitting in a different
pocket at a different angle. Stage 4 stays exploratory because there is no ground truth,
not because there is no signal.

### Why consistency is not correctness — made quantitative

If ω_v carries strong power at the stride rate (half the step rate), then sampling it once
per step lands on alternating half-cycles *whatever its physical origin and whatever its
phase*. A pure sinusoid at f_step/2 sampled every 1/f_step alternates perfectly while
encoding nothing about laterality. Here ω_v has a stride/step power ratio with a median of
2.1, reaching 59. The three controls (`random`, phase-randomised `surrogate`, `mid_step`)
exist to bound exactly that, and the mid-step control frequently *beats* the real contact
sample — on the reference subject, 0.949 versus 0.539.

### Pocket versus sacrum, mechanistically

At the sacrum the theory is testing pelvic transverse rotation: a small, symmetric,
midline signal that genuinely reverses between left and right stance. At the front trouser
pocket the sensor is on **one thigh**. The dominant contribution to ω_v is that leg's own
hip rotation, so what alternates is mostly *ipsilateral swing versus contralateral swing* —
a much larger signal (ω_v RMS measured here: 0.88–3.64 rad/s, median 2.18) with a different
physical origin. It happens to alternate with the correct period, which is precisely why
alternation consistency cannot distinguish it from the effect being sought.

Two further consequences unique to the pocket: the sign convention is tied to *which leg
carries the phone*, and the dataset never recorded which pocket each subject used — so even
if the sign separated the sides perfectly, the mapping from sign to anatomical side is
undetermined per subject. This is not something a better algorithm fixes.

## 6. Where the pocket and 50 Hz are doing load-bearing work

| # | Dependency | Severity at sacrum / 100–200 Hz |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Stage 4 in its entirety.** Depends on contact timing we cannot recover, on a sensor on one thigh, and on a pocket side that was never recorded. | **Rebuild from scratch.** Nothing here transfers. Treat the code as an instrument for asking the question, not as an answer. |
| 2 | **Detected peak is a phase marker, not initial contact.** At 50 Hz the impact transient that marks true contact is above the usable band; the ±10%-of-step contact window is only **5 samples**. | **High.** At 200 Hz the transient is resolvable and true contact becomes detectable — that is a *different algorithm*, not a retuned one. Everything phase-sensitive is blocked until then. |
| 3 | **Low-pass at 1.5 × f_step**, chosen against pocket harmonic content (2nd harmonic median 11.4%, max 67% of fundamental). | **Medium.** Re-run the cutoff sweep at the sacrum; harmonic structure will differ. At 200 Hz this band also discards the impact transient you will want for loading rate later. |
| 4 | **Forward axis via step-band cross-spectrum.** Beats broadband PCA 14.2 : 2.1 on conditioning *at the pocket*. | **Medium, benign.** Fore-aft should dominate more at the sacrum, so plain PCA may become viable. Re-measure the eigenvalue ratio; do not assume either way. |
| 5 | **Tracking frame mode.** Exists because the thigh swings 15–30°. | **Low.** Sacrum tilt is far smaller, so a static frame may become valid; tracking still works and costs nothing. Safe either way. |
| 6 | **Stride-lag periodicity acceptance** rather than step-lag, forced by pocket step asymmetry (symmetry index down to 0.05). | **Low, and correct anyway.** The stride is gait's true repeat period regardless of placement. |
| 7 | **Steady-state trim threshold** (0.5 × median 1 s RMS) calibrated on pocket signal amplitudes. | **Low for handling, high for walking.** The gap between running and handling is wide. But a walk sits at ~0.5 × running RMS — on the threshold — so the trimmer cannot separate walking from running, and it keeps only the *longest* active segment: a 30 min run with a walk break at minute 10 keeps ten minutes. Every active segment and the fraction kept are now reported (`n_active_segments`, `kept_fraction_of_active`; 14/48 trials here have more than one, min kept 48%), and a walk inside the kept segment is caught downstream as a detection gap. A production run needs per-segment analysis, not this trimmer. |

Explicitly **not** load-bearing, and measured rather than asserted:

- **Orientation.** Rotating every sensor vector by an arbitrary rotation changes cadence by
  **0.00 spm** and preserves step count in **144/144** rotated runs across all 48 trials.
  Exact, not approximate.
- **Sample rate.** Resampled to 25 / 100 / 200 Hz, cadence moves by a median of
  0.032–0.055% and at worst 0.787%. The number 50 appears once in the package
  (`loader.DEFAULT_FS_HZ`) and only as a default argument.
- One genuine rate cliff was found and now fails loudly: clamping the low-pass to the
  Nyquist cap could push the step fundamental out of its own passband while `low < high`
  still held, producing a filter that removed the component being detected and reporting no
  error. It binds when `fs < 2.5 × f_step` and now raises. (A narrower silent clamp remains
  between `2.5 × f_step` and `3.75 × f_step`, where the low-pass is pinned to `0.4 × fs`
  rather than `1.5 × f_step`; it cannot be reached at running cadence and is pinned by an
  xfail test.)
- `fs_hz` is an input assertion, and getting it wrong rescales every result linearly while
  both cadence estimators keep agreeing with each other. `steps.check_sample_rate` now tests
  the claimed rate against the data — comparing the strongest spectral peak inside the
  plausible human step band against the strongest peak outside it — and
  `diagnose_cadence` reports the cause `sample_rate` rather than blaming the runner. Across
  all 48 trials at the true rate the ratio never exceeds 0.476; presenting 50 Hz data as
  100 Hz gives 13.2 and as 200 Hz gives 647. **Blind spot, stated rather than papered
  over: understating the rate by ~2× is undetectable**, because it moves a 2.7 Hz
  fundamental to 1.36 Hz, still inside the human band and indistinguishable from a slow
  walker.

## 7. What this dataset fundamentally cannot tell you

1. **Anything requiring true initial contact.** Contact time, loading rate, braking
   impulse, vertical oscillation, flight time. 50 Hz on a soft-tissue-coupled pocket does
   not resolve the impact transient. (Not built, per your instructions — this is *why* that
   was the right call.)
2. **Whether left/right discrimination works.** No labels exist. The best this data
   supports is what is reported: alternation consistency, its null distributions, and the
   observation that the result is dominated by an unrecoverable phase offset.
3. **Whether the forward axis is really forward.** No motion-capture reference, no heading
   ground truth. The axis is well determined and its anatomical identity is an inference.
4. **Anything about the sacrum.** All 48 trials are pocket. Every placement-related number
   here is a pocket number. The cross-checks are the transferable artifact, not the values.
5. **Sampling integrity.** No timestamps. Dropped samples, jitter and drift are invisible,
   so no claim about timing robustness can be validated here.
6. **Within-runner change over time.** One session per subject, no repeated days. Pace
   normalisation and changepoint detection are unbuildable — as you said. (Not built.)
7. **Anything at pace variation.** Trials are single-bout steady jogging, 15–111 s. There
   is no pace ladder, no fatigue arc, no terrain change.
8. **Subject 4's cadence is a data point, not a bug.** Worth keeping in mind that a 150 spm
   floor will flag genuine runners.

## 9. Audit — what changed and why

A verification pass was run against the code as committed: every number in this report was
re-derived, the report tables were regenerated from a clean checkout (byte-identical), and
the algorithms were probed with synthetic signals whose true answer is known. What follows
is what did not survive, and what was done about it. Every item has a regression test in
`tests/test_audit_regressions.py` that ran red against the old code.

### Numbers that moved

| quantity | before | after | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stage-2 verdicts | 2 `ok` / 46 `vertical_only` | **0 / 48** | the two `ok`s were chance passes of the ML check on undetermined stride axes |
| ML cross-check | passes 2/48 | supported 1 / undetermined 3 / unsupported 44 | requires a determined stride axis; computed on tracking-resolved channels |
| step/stride axis angle | mean 20.5°, median 16.5° | mean 27.5°, median 26.4° | computed on tracking-resolved channels, not the trial-mean plane |

### Defects fixed in the code

- `verify_frame` gates `ok` on forward-axis stability and refuses `ok` when stability could
  not be measured (< 8 s). `mediolateral_cross_check` requires a determined stride axis and
  runs on the tracking-resolved horizontal channels. `build_frame` measures f_step on the
  tracking vertical. `resolve` stays orthonormal (no NaN) where up ∥ forward, and the count
  of such samples is reported.
- `_principal_axis_2d` distinguishes *no power* (NaN, undetermined) from *single-axis power*
  (+inf, perfectly determined); a previous fix had folded both into NaN, which failed the
  cleanest possible input.
- `verify_vertical_periodicity` takes its autocorrelation on [0.3, 4] × f_step and reads it
  at the exact fractional lag, which also makes regularity rate-agnostic.
- `dsp._nperseg` tiles the record (even segment length), so `welch` no longer discards a
  trailing partial segment.
- `steady_state_segment` refuses non-finite input (a NaN used to disable the trim silently),
  returns every active segment, and reports the fraction of active time the longest one
  holds.
- `cadence_summary` reports the largest interval in step periods and a `detection_gap`
  flag; `diagnose_cadence` attributes a gap to the trial, a stride-subharmonic lock-on to
  `ambiguous`, a missing spectral estimate to the trial (it printed "disagrees by nan%"),
  and `flagged` is now exactly `cause != "none"` (a low stride regularity used to give
  `cause="trial"` with `flagged=False`).
- `pipeline.run_trial` refuses a file with non-finite samples or a broken index up front,
  naming the problem; the integrity record was computed and never read.
- `scripts/run_all.py` no longer crashes in `print_verdicts` when any trial raised (one bad
  file killed the summary after the CSVs were written); failed trials are listed by name.
- Plots read the steady-state window, threshold, cadence band and smoothing window from
  `steps` instead of repeating the numbers; the "5-step median" label was wrong (6).
- `tests/conftest.py` makes real-trial tests skip, not error, without the dataset; the
  synthetic tests — the ones that check the mathematics — run anywhere. 106 tests, 2 xfail
  (the biased autocorrelation normaliser and the silent Nyquist clamp, both documented and
  neither reachable at running cadence).

### Verified and unchanged

Sign conventions (up = −ĝ; ML = up × forward points left; ω_v positive = turning left);
rotation invariance under named placements (upside-down, yawed 180°, on its side) as well as
random ones; the tracking frame's per-sample orthonormality; the step-band estimator's use of
the real co-spectrum (PSD by Cauchy–Schwarz); the periodicity band edges; the steady-state
trim on handling transients; the surrogate's spectrum and variance preservation; sample-rate
plausibility at 200 Hz with a synthetic impact transient; determinism of every seeded path;
and timing at production scale (a 30 min, 200 Hz record runs every stage in under 2 s each,
peak memory under 20 MB).

### What remains

- The trimmer keeps one segment and cannot tell walking from running (§6, row 7). Within-run
  analysis needs windowed cadence over every active segment; the raw material
  (`cadence_series`) exists and nothing consumes it.
- Stage 4 has no null that a step-locked, lateral-blind signal would fail. Until there are
  labels, no number here can be read as laterality.
- The autocorrelation normaliser is biased by (n − k)/n (xfail); it deflates stride
  regularity about twice as much as step regularity on short records.
- `check_sample_rate` cannot see a ~2× *understatement* of the rate (§6).
- Nothing about the sacrum, true initial contact, or any metric beyond cadence is testable
  on this data (§7).
