# Gait signal core — findings

Scope: **48 jog trials** (24 subjects × trials 9 and 16), MotionSense `A_DeviceMotion_data`,
50 Hz nominal, iPhone 6s in the front trouser pocket — plus synthetic sessions written in the
exact format the **BaselineLogger** iOS app documents, so the two halves of the system are
connected on the app's format. **No session recorded by a device has been run through the
pipeline yet**; that is the first thing to do with a phone in hand.

**Provenance — what is traceable and what is not.** Most numbers below are per-trial columns
in `reports/pipeline_results.csv` and `reports/invariance.csv`, regenerated by
`scripts/run_all.py` and `scripts/run_invariance_checks.py`: cadence and its diagnosis, the
stage-2 verdicts and the forward-sign criteria, tilt, step and stride regularity, the symmetry
index, bouts, alternation and its null models, the quality roll-up, and both invariance
sweeps. Re-running those two scripts reproduces every one of them, and the notebook prints
its stage-4 numbers from the CSV rather than transcribing them. Four groups are **not** in
either CSV and come from one-off analyses over the same trials: the broadband-PCA vs
step-band conditioning comparison (§3), the second-harmonic power ratio (§4), the
static-vs-tracking vertical correlation (§3), and the low-pass cutoff sweep (§4). The
harmonic-rejection claim is pinned by
`tests/test_failure_modes.py::test_lowpass_rejects_the_second_harmonic_on_filter_shape_alone`;
the rest are reproducible only by hand. Treat them as measured but not continuously verified.

## 2. Stage 1 — loader and explorer

Layout was read from the dataset's own README and verified on disk before any loader code
was written: `A_DeviceMotion_data/<activity>_<trial>/sub_<subject>.csv`, jog = trials 9 and
16, 24 subjects each, 12 signal columns plus an unnamed integer row counter.

**Polarity.** CoreMotion's `userAcceleration` is the negative of the kinematic acceleration
(fix #9). `Trial.user_accel` converts it; `Trial.df` keeps the file's values;
`Trial.user_accel_as_recorded` returns the raw columns for anyone comparing against the file.
Everything downstream is in kinematic polarity: positive vertical is the body accelerating
away from the earth.

**MotionSense has no timestamp column.** Its time index is `arange(n) / fs_hz` — a grid we
impose — and dropped samples, jitter and drift are undetectable. Every MotionSense integrity
record carries `has_timestamp_column: False` and `timestamp_irregularities_detectable: False`.

**BaselineLogger sessions do have timestamps, and the loader uses them for everything.** `t`
is a per-sample hardware timestamp on the app's monotonic clock, kept on the app's origin
(`integrity["t_start_s"]` is the first kept sample's time, so markers and `gps.csv` align).
The sample rate is measured (median interval), jitter is measured (robust spread), step
times are read from the hardware clock rather than the index grid, and every irregularity
is counted independently of `session.json`:

- gaps (> 3× median, the same multiple as the app's GapTracker — the same *rule* on a steady
  stream, not the same algorithm across a rate change; `tests/test_gaptracker_port.py`
  pins both),
- samples dropped below the gap threshold (> 1.5× median; one dropped sample is 2×, which the
  gap rule never sees),
- duplicated or reordered samples (delta ≤ 0), and a non-finite timestamp (refused),
- an unfinished session (`inProgress`), rows the app lost to disk (`csvRowsLost`), and the
  app's sample count against the rows actually in the file — the last two are blockers,
- GPS: the cached pre-session fix, invalid speeds and invalid accuracies are dropped and
  counted; the median and 95th-percentile horizontal accuracy are reported, and a median
  above 20 m (Precise Location off) is a caveat.

A gapped session is **refused by default** (`on_gap="raise"`), because every filter downstream
assumes a uniform grid. `on_gap="longest"` keeps the longest clean stretch and reports what
was cut, and a cut record is never `clean`. Dropped samples are gated proportionally: under
0.1 % of samples is a caveat (the filtered waveform has a discontinuity at each drop; cadence
is unaffected because step times come from the clock), above it a refusal.

Two smaller traps on MotionSense, both handled: `gravity` magnitude is unit to 2e-5 by
CoreMotion's construction (a corruption check, not evidence the fusion tracks), and every
trial contains handling transients at one or both ends — 88 s across the dataset is trimmed.

Measured durations are 15.3–110.7 s; six trials are under 20 s.

## 3. Stage 2 — orientation

### Vertical: solid, but only because it tracks

Instantaneous gravity direction wanders **15.4° median, 30.9° at p95, 53° max** from the
trial-mean direction within a single trial — the thigh swinging. A single trial-constant
rotation is invalid on **0/48** trials. The default frame mode is therefore `tracking`: `up`
from instantaneous gravity at every sample, forward re-projected into each sample's horizontal
plane. Static and tracking vertical acceleration correlate 0.93–1.00, so the static frame is
not catastrophic for amplitude; it is simply not defensible as a frame.

**Against ground truth.** On synthetic body-frame signals rotated by arbitrary rotations,
including upside-down and 180° yaw, `build_frame` recovers `up` to < 0.5° and `forward` to
< 3° with the correct sign, and the resolved vertical channel equals the true one to 1e-9.
The round-2 reviewer repeated this under pitch ±90°, roll 90° and six Haar-random rotations,
under both polarities: 0.0–0.8°.

**What tracking cannot do.** The re-projection corrects pitch and roll wobble (≤ 0.002 g
error on every channel for 5° wobble) but is blind to rotation *about* the vertical, because
gravity does not change under it. Pelvic transverse rotation (±5–10° once per stride at the
sacrum) leaks fore-aft into the mediolateral channel by sin(yaw): ~13 % of the true ML RMS on
a pelvis-like synthetic, scaling with pace. Any ML quantity is a proxy until the yaw is
estimated from the gyro and removed. Documented in `resolve`.

### Forward: determined; anatomically forward only where shown

| estimator | median eigenvalue ratio | well-conditioned (≥ 4.0) |
|---|---|---|
| broadband PCA (as specified) | 2.11 (one-off analysis) | **3/48** |
| step-band cross-spectrum (default) | 16.10 (`forward_eig_ratio` column) | **45/48** |

Broadband horizontal acceleration at the pocket is nearly isotropic. The step-band estimator
restricts to a narrow band around the step fundamental and is far better conditioned; the
real part of the cross-spectral matrix is the right object (a fixed axis produces only
in-phase co-spectrum; a circular motion gives ratio 1.00 and is correctly refused).

**The 180° sign — fixes #3 and #16.** The sign is set by the phase relation at the centre of
mass: braking coincides with *rising* vertical acceleration, so `corr(a_fwd, d a_vert/dt) < 0`.
It is invariant to input polarity and recovers the true direction to < 1° on synthetics under
every rotation tried. Confidence means |corr| ≥ 0.1 and the same sign on both halves of the
record: **37/48** on the pocket data. That is a centre-of-mass argument proven on synthetic
centre-of-mass signals; at the thigh it is an inference from the same stance mechanics, not a
validated one. The old "impact" criterion (fore-aft *at* the vertical peak) is reported but
never gated on: on centre-of-mass synthetics its statistic is within ±0.012 under 16 rotations,
and on the pocket data it agrees with the phase criterion on 28/48 — a coin flip. Requiring
agreement with a null made "confident" unreachable at the production placement, which is why
round 2 removed it. Nothing in stages 3 or 4 depends on the sign; it sets plot polarity and the
left/right naming of the ML axis, and where it is unresolved the roll-up says so.

### Mediolateral: the cross-check fails, and it is a real check

At the trunk, fore-aft acceleration is driven once per **step**, mediolateral once per
**stride**, so the two horizontal power axes should sit ~90° apart. Measured: **19.6° mean**
(median 15.7°) — nearly parallel. The prediction fails on 46/48. On the synthetic sacrum-like
signal the same check passes at > 80°, which is what it should do; keep it as a gate.

### The finding that drives everything downstream

The strongest autocorrelation lag of vertical acceleration is at **two step periods (the
stride), not one, on all 48 trials**. Step-lag regularity 0.09–0.82; stride-lag 0.68–0.92
(unbiased estimate, local maximum near each lag). Symmetry index median **0.52**, range
0.11–0.96, with the band's low edge at 0.25 × f_step so the stride subharmonic that carries
the asymmetry is no longer half-removed (fix #17). Consecutive steps are not interchangeable
— a sensor on **one** thigh. The acceptance test is applied at the stride lag.

## 4. Stage 3 — running-state, steps, cadence

### Running-state detection — fixes #2, #10, #11

Acceleration magnitude (rotation- and polarity-invariant) in 1 s windows; a window is running
when its RMS is ≥ 0.5× the record median **and ≥ 0.2 g**. The floor is what stops a record
that is mostly standing from having its noise as the median (fix #10); its value comes from
the data: standing and sitting never exceed 0.13 g, every locomotion class sits above 0.23 g.
Sub-threshold dips shorter than 3 s are bridged; a real stop splits the record into bouts,
**every bout ≥ 5 s is analysed, and the cadence is pooled over their regular strides**. On
MotionSense one trial has two bouts; on a product run a traffic light no longer costs half
the summary.

### Filter cutoffs and why

Both cutoffs are multiples of the estimated step frequency, so the filter is identical in
relative terms at any cadence and any rate (the one silent exception, a clamp between
2.5× and 3.75× f_step, cannot be reached at running cadence and is pinned by an xfail test).

- **High-pass 0.7 × f_step**: between the stride subharmonic and the fundamental; ~12 dB on
  the subharmonic for 0.25 dB on the fundamental.
- **Low-pass 1.5 × f_step**: the second harmonic carries 11.6 % of fundamental power at the
  median and 67 % at most, and passing it puts a second crest in every step cycle (2.0
  peaks/cycle at 3× vs 1.0 at 1.5×). With the peak-spacing rule disabled, 1.5× is unchanged
  while 2.0× degrades ×2.4 and 3.0× by ×30: the filter, not the spacing rule, does the work.
- Zero-phase `sosfiltfilt`; second-order sections.

Peak spacing 0.6 step periods; prominence 0.5 × robust MAD-sigma, with a zero or near-zero
sigma treated as **no signal**. A global prominence loses steps in a portion of a bout whose
amplitude drops to a fifth of the rest; that shows up as irregular strides (a caveat above
10 %), not as a silent miss.

### Cadence statistics — fix #12

All from **stride** intervals. Headline cadence = 120 / mean regular stride, regular meaning
within ±30 % of the median stride; a missed step (1.5×), a double detection (0.5×) or a pause
inside a bridged bout (≥ 2×) drops out of both numerator and denominator. The whole-span rate a
step counter would report is kept as `cadence_spm_span` (biased low by any pause). `cadence_cv`
is the stride-interval CV over regular strides.

### Validation, all 24 subjects

Cadence **149.1–183.8 spm**, median 164.9. **47/48 inside 150–190 spm.** Detector vs spectral
seed within **4.1 %** on every trial (ratio 0.965–1.041). Stride CV median 0.039, max 0.067;
irregular strides median 0 %, max 6.2 %; smoothed-cadence spread within a bout median 5.6 %,
max 11.6 % (the mixed-gait rule fires above 20 %). A subject's two jog trials give cadences
differing by a median of 1.7 spm (max 6.7).

| flagged | detected | spectral | cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| `jog_9/sub_4` | 149.1 spm | 148.9 spm | **trial** — the estimators agree to 0 %; this runner really is just under 150 |

Attribution is four-way: `none` / `sample_rate` / `algorithm` / `trial`, in this order of
checks: the spectral rate check (out-of-band/in-band peak ratio; ≤ 0.44 across all 48 trials
at the true rate, 15 at a 2× overstatement, 1000 at 4×); a NaN cadence; stride regularity;
**a harmonic-ambiguous seed** (fix #14; 0/48); **more than one gait in the bout** (fix #13;
0/48); **the 210 spm ceiling** (fix #15); then the detector/spectral ratio. The two estimators
are not independent — the detector's band is built around the spectral peak — so the ratio
catches the detector missing alternate steps or splitting crests inside the band it was given,
and a wrong seed has to be caught at the source, which the ambiguity check does. Understating
the rate by ~2× remains undetectable (2.7 Hz becomes 1.36 Hz, inside the human band), and
overstating it by less than ~1.25× is indistinguishable from a fast runner. For logger
sessions the rate is measured, so these guard only against a corrupted `t` column.

### One artifact you must not read as a gait metric

Detected step intervals alternate short–long by up to **24 %** (median 8 %); real runners
show 1–3 %. It is a detector timing artifact — different waveform shapes on alternate steps
shift the crest of the band-passed fundamental — and it correlates at **r = −0.61** with the
amplitude-domain symmetry index. Reported as an absolute value (its sign depends on which step
lands at index 0), and the reason every cadence statistic is stride-based and the smoother
uses an even window.

## 5. Stage 4 — left/right (exploratory; no ground truth; no verdict)

**No left/right labels exist, and the dataset never recorded which pocket held the phone.**
Labels are `A`/`B`, a parity sequence relative to an unknown anchor: one sign glitch inverts
every later label, so they are for plotting and must never be aggregated per side. No
accuracy is claimed, and since round 2 **no side verdict is emitted at all** (fix #18).

### Result 1 — at the step marker, it does not work here

| quantity | median over 48 trials |
|---|---|
| alternation at the detected step marker | **0.671** |
| phase-randomised surrogate null | 0.562 |
| excess over surrogate | +0.118 (> 0.10 on 24/48) |
| steps whose contact sample is under a quarter of the signal's RMS | 28 % |

Only 9/48 reach 0.90. The marker moved from mid-flight to mid-stance with the polarity fix,
which is why these numbers differ from round 1's (0.486 / 0.551 / −0.027); the conclusion does
not. And the instant the theory names is a poor one at any placement: the gyro measures the
*derivative* of a rotation that reverses near contact, so a hard sign read there sits at a
zero crossing by construction. The cluster separation between the two sign groups (median
d = 3.0) exceeds the 2.65 that Gaussian noise gives by only 0.36.

### Result 2 — the number is dominated by sampling phase

Alternation swings from near zero to near one across sampling phase within a trial (median
range 0.74). Best-phase median 0.939.

### Result 3 — there is structure locked to the detector, and that is all this shows

Against a sweep-and-maximise surrogate null, **45/48 trials exceed the null's 95th percentile
at their best phase**, and the best phase recurs within a runner above chance (exact-bin
agreement 11/24 against a chance 2.4; within one bin 17/24 against 7.2). Round 1 read this as
"structure beyond the spectrum". Round 2 showed what it actually is: the surrogate destroys
phase-locking to the gait cycle the steps were detected from, which *every* real ω_v has. A
fake ω_v built as a pure function of the detected step times — no left/right content at all
— beats the surrogate on 11 of 12 real trials once the steps jitter as real ones do, and a
jittered laterality-free synthetic session beats it in the pipeline (pinned by test).
Laterality *is* a gait-locked stride-rate oscillation, so no signal-only null separates the two.
The excess over the surrogate measures phase-locking to the detector — which the phone-leg's
own motion produces by itself — and is never turned into a verdict.

### Pocket versus sacrum, mechanically

At the sacrum the theory tests pelvic transverse rotation, a small midline signal. At the
pocket ω_v is dominated by the phone leg's own motion (RMS 0.88–3.65 rad/s, median 2.25;
stride/step power ratio median 1.7, up to 58) — its source is not separable in this data: the
vertical-axis rate is 60–75 % of the total rotation-rate magnitude, several times what hip
axial rotation alone would give, so "hip rotation" is a guess, not a measurement. It
alternates with the right period for the wrong reason. At the sacrum a rebuild should read a
stance-integrated quantity rather than a point sign, and will still need a ground-truth
anchor (instrumented insole, video, a scripted single-leg event at the start) to map A/B onto
left/right; no signal-only method supplies that.

## 6. Where placement and rate are doing load-bearing work

| # | Dependency | At sacrum / 100–200 Hz |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Stage 4 entirely.** Contact timing we cannot recover; a sensor on one thigh; a pocket side never recorded; the named instant is a zero crossing. | **Rebuild from scratch**, with a stance-integrated statistic and an anchor. |
| 2 | **Detected peak is a phase marker, not initial contact.** At 50 Hz the contact window is 5 samples. | **High.** At 200 Hz the transient is resolvable and true contact becomes detectable — a different algorithm, not a retune. The raw stream carries CoreMotion's sign; convert it as the loader does. |
| 3 | **Low-pass at 1.5 × f_step**, chosen against pocket harmonic content. | **Medium.** Re-run the sweep at the sacrum; at 200 Hz this band also discards the impact transient wanted for loading rate. A second harmonic *stronger* than the fundamental would trip the sample-rate check (pocket max 0.67); unmeasured at the sacrum. |
| 4 | **Forward via step-band cross-spectrum**; sign via the phase criterion. | **Medium.** The phase criterion is *designed* for the centre of mass and proven only on synthetic data there; the step-band estimator's conditioning is a pocket measurement. Re-measure both; a run-start check (the mean horizontal acceleration over the first seconds points forward) would give an independent sign on real sessions. |
| 5 | **Tracking frame mode**, because the thigh swings 15–30°. | **Low.** Sacrum tilt is far smaller; tracking still works. Yaw stays invisible to it. |
| 6 | **Stride-lag acceptance**, forced by pocket step asymmetry. | **Low, and correct anyway.** |
| 7 | **Steady-state trim** (0.5 × median RMS, 0.2 g floor, 3 s bridge). | **Low.** The floor separates locomotion from none at any placement; the 0.5 fraction sits at walking's level at the pocket (walking ≈ 0.49 × jogging RMS) and should separate cleanly at the trunk (≈ 0.3×). Re-check the bridge length against real stops. |
| 8 | **Mixed-gait rule at 20 %**, calibrated on 48 steady pocket trials (max spread 11.6 %). | **Low.** Interval sessions (hard/easy ≈ 12 %) stay under it; re-check on real sessions. |

Explicitly **not** load-bearing, and measured:

- **Orientation.** Arbitrary rotations of every sensor vector change cadence by **0.00 spm**
  and preserve step count in **144/144** rotated runs. Exact.
- **Sample rate.** Resampled to 25 / 100 / 200 Hz, cadence moves by a median of 0.03 % and at
  worst **0.33 % (25 Hz), 0.24 % (100 Hz), 0.27 % (200 Hz)**. The number 50 appears once in
  the package and only as a default. Logger sessions never use it: the rate is measured.
- **Polarity.** Cadence, spectra and axes are invariant to it; the conversion at the loader
  boundary changed no cadence.

## 7. What this data fundamentally cannot tell you

1. **Anything requiring true initial contact** — contact time, loading rate, braking impulse,
   vertical oscillation. Not built; this is why.
2. **Whether left/right discrimination works.** No labels, and no signal-only null can stand
   in for them.
3. **Whether the forward axis is really forward on a runner.** Proven on synthetic signals;
   no motion-capture reference for real ones.
4. **Anything about the sacrum from MotionSense.** All 48 trials are pocket. The cross-checks
   are the transferable artifact, not the values.
5. **Sampling integrity of MotionSense.** No timestamps. (Logger sessions: measurable, and
   measured.)
6. **Within-runner change over time.** One session per subject.
7. **Anything at pace variation.** Single steady bouts, 15–111 s. `gps.csv` carries speed with
   its accuracy, but nothing consumes it yet, and coarse fixes are only flagged.
8. **Subject 4's cadence is a data point, not a bug.** A 150 spm floor will flag real runners.
9. **Whether the app records what these tests assume.** The format is proven on synthetic
   sessions; no device session exists in either repository.

---

## 8. Not built, per instructions

No vertical oscillation, braking impulse, loading rate, ground contact time, or asymmetry
(they need true initial contact and the sample rate MotionSense lacks). No pace model,
residuals, within-run trajectory, or changepoint detection (they need multi-session per-runner
data at known pace; the logger records what they would need). No report UI. The audit brief
describes these as intended product features; they have no code in either repository, and this
report does not audit what does not exist. The mixed-gait rule is a gate, not a segmenter: a
bout that holds two gaits is refused, not split.

## 9. How the audit was done, and its limits

- **Python:** every finding was reproduced with code before being fixed, and every fix has a
  regression test — **138 tests, 1 deliberate xfail** (the unreachable Nyquist clamp). All 48
  trials, both invariance sweeps and the notebook run with zero errors.
- **Swift:** **no Swift toolchain was available.** Every iOS change was made by static review,
  the tracker arithmetic is validated by a line-for-line Python port that now lives in
  `tests/test_gaptracker_port.py`, and symbol/call-site consistency was checked by hand. The
  changes have not been compiled or run on a device. Build in Xcode and do a locked-screen
  test run before trusting a long session.
- **Adversarial fan-out:** six independent reviewer lenses (Swift sensors, Swift lifecycle,
  Python orientation, steps, lateral, and end-to-end integration) each completed; 51 findings
  in all. Two refuters per finding were launched three times and ran into a subagent session
  limit each time; 31 verdicts completed, all on the Swift findings: they confirmed fixes
  #5–#8 as present at HEAD and left the four low-severity items in row iOS-2, which are fixed.
  **No verifier ran on any Python finding**; each of those was reproduced by hand, from the
  reviewer's own scripts, before being accepted or fixed (rows #9–#20). One finding was not
  accepted as stated: the sample-rate check's peak-height comparison (§6 row 3) is documented
  rather than changed, because no data here says what the second harmonic does at the sacrum.
- **Unverified claims that remain:** whether When-In-Use location authorization alone keeps
  capture alive after screen lock on iOS 16; whether any iPhone delivers 200 Hz through
  `CMMotionManager`; whether iOS ever changes CoreMotion's delivery rate mid-session (the
  GapTracker lag matters only if it does); the 3 s bridge and the 20 % mixed-gait rule against
  real stops and real sessions; and the whole app-to-pipeline path on a real recording.
